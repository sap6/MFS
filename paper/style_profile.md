# Writing style for the paper (taken from the thesis)

Used for the humanising pass of 18 November 2026. The author asked: "Don't
rewrite the paper ... just do a humanising pass. Use my thesis to get my style
of writing." The structure, content and numbers stay the same. Only the
sentences change.

## The thesis voice

- A guiding "we" that walks the reader through the work: "We begin by
  focusing on the familiar domain of a unit sphere", "Now we present our
  results", "First, we investigate", "We then calculate", "In this section,
  we implement the same problem ... but for a unit cube".
- Pointers back and forward: "Recall that ...", "Note that ...", "As usual,
  ...", "as shown below", "the same plane wave test used in Section ...".
- Reasons stated with "because", "since", "so that", "which in turn",
  "As a result", "Consequently", "Thus", "Therefore".
- Contrasts marked: "However,", "In contrast,", "Conversely,", "Similarly,".
- Medium to long sentences (20 to 35 words) with a lead-in phrase, mixed
  with a short one for the main point. Not runs of short declaratives.
- Formulas explained in words straight after them: "where ...", "Here, ...",
  "Specifically, ...".
- Parenthetical asides: "(also known as charge points)", "(i.e. ...)".
- Plain expectations and outcomes: "Therefore, a reliable numerical
  approximation should yield ...", "This shows that high accuracy is
  attainable even with modest discretisation", "errors on the order of".
- British spelling (discretisation, behaviour, polarisation), "the MFS".

## Thesis phrases reused where accurate

"The general idea of the MFS is to approximate the solution ... by a linear
combination of fundamental solutions of the governing differential
equation"; "A well-documented disadvantage of the MFS is that this system of
equations is often ill-conditioned"; "To lift the scalar solution to a
vector solution, we follow ..."; "Because this Green's function takes the
form of a rank-2 tensor ... it is often referred to as a dyadic Green's
function"; "We begin by focusing on the familiar domain of a unit sphere";
"impinges from the positive z-axis"; "Here, the conductor occupies a bounded
domain with surface Γ, and the physical requirement is that ..."; "At each
boundary point a local tangent basis is computed"; "Until now, ... The
smooth curvature of the sphere ensures that the surface normals change
gradually ... In contrast, ... Its flat faces, combined with sharp edges and
abrupt corners, produce discontinuities in the surface normals"; "a
familiar test case in computational electromagnetics"; "In conclusion, ...".

## Kept out (from the humanizer rules)

No em dashes; no "novel", "robust", "comprehensive", "crucial",
"underscore", "highlight" (verb), "showcase", "pivotal", "intricate"; no
"-ing" tails that add a verdict; no stacked hedges; no promotional thesis
phrases ("highly sought after", "daunting", "truly meshless", "exceptional
capability"); one term per concept; every claim keeps its number.

## What made the draft read as machine-written

Runs of short declarative sentences that all begin with "The"; few
connectives, so the reader has to supply the logic; many coined names
introduced with "We call ..."; set phrases such as "is a different matter",
"It remains to", "thus shows", "Our contributions are the following",
"Before we conclude, we state the limits of what we have shown".
